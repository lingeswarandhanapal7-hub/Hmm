# Tenant Rights — Security Deposit Refund & Permissible Deductions

## Category: Housing & Tenancy Rights
## Citation: Model Tenancy Act, Section 13 & Transfer of Property Act
## Statutory Urgency: Medium

### Statutory Ceiling on Security Deposits
Under the Model Tenancy Act, security deposits for residential rental premises are legally capped at a maximum of **two months' rent** (or up to six months' rent for non-residential commercial premises). Excessive demands of 6-10 months' rent are non-compliant with standard model guidelines.

### Mandatory Refund Timeline:
- The security deposit must be refunded to the tenant within **30 days** of vacating and handing over vacant peaceful possession of the rental premises, subject to legitimate deductions.

### Permissible vs Non-Permissible Deductions:
1. **Permissible Deductions**:
   - Actual unpaid rent or pending utility bills (electricity, water, piped gas).
   - Proven physical damage to fixtures or property caused by tenant negligence.
2. **Strictly Non-Permissible Deductions**:
   - Normal wear and tear (e.g. slight fading of wall paint, minor scuffs from standard habitation, aging washers).
   - Mandatory repainting deductions without clause agreed in advance or inspection evidence.
   - Forfeiture of deposit without itemized repair invoices.

### Action Plan if Landlord Withholds Deposit:
- Demand an itemized written statement of deductions accompanied by contractor bills within 7 days.
- Send a formal legal demand notice via Speed Post or registered legal email citing Section 13 of the Tenancy Act.
- Approach the District Rent Authority for summary recovery with interest if unpaid after 30 days.
