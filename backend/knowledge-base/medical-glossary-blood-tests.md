# Medical Lab Report Glossary — Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Lipid Profile

## Category: Medical Knowledge & Diagnostic Glossaries
## Clinical Safety Notice: Explanatory reference only. Never state a clinical diagnosis or treatment course. Direct patient to review with a licensed medical practitioner.
## Urgency: Routine / Informational (unless flagged critical by laboratory panic values)

### Complete Blood Count (CBC) Parameters Explained
- **Hemoglobin (Hb)**: The oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells. Normal range ~12-16 g/dL for women, 13.5-17.5 g/dL for men. Lower values indicate potential anemia or blood loss; higher values can occur in dehydration or smoking.
- **WBC (White Blood Cells) / Total Leukocyte Count (TLC)**: Immune cells that fight bacterial or viral infections. Typical range 4,000 - 11,000 /mcL. Elevated levels often reflect natural immune response to infection or inflammation.
- **Platelet Count**: Cell fragments essential for blood clotting and stopping bleeds. Normal range 150,000 - 450,000 /mcL. Low counts (thrombocytopenia) require caution regarding bruising or bleeding.
- **Neutrophils / Lymphocytes**: Major sub-types of white blood cells. Neutrophils rise in bacterial infections; lymphocytes rise in viral responses.

### Lipid (Cholesterol) Profile Parameters Explained
- **Total Cholesterol**: Combined measurement of cholesterol circulating in blood. Desirable level: < 200 mg/dL.
- **LDL (Low-Density Lipoprotein)**: Commonly termed "bad cholesterol" because excess accumulates in arterial walls. Optimal target is typically < 100 mg/dL (or < 70 mg/dL for high cardiovascular risk individuals).
- **HDL (High-Density Lipoprotein)**: Referred to as "protective / good cholesterol" because it carries excess lipids back to the liver. Desirable level: > 40 mg/dL for men, > 50 mg/dL for women.
- **Triglycerides**: Storage fats in the bloodstream derived from excess dietary calories/carbohydrates. Normal range: < 150 mg/dL.

### Patient Safety Action Points:
- Borderline or flagged numbers do NOT equal disease without clinical correlation by your treating physician.
- Fasting status (9-12 hours) significantly alters triglyceride and glucose numbers; verify with doctor if test was taken non-fasting.
- Formulate 2-3 specific questions for your doctor regarding lifestyle, diet, or repeat testing schedule.
