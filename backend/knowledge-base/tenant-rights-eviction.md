# Tenant Rights & Protection Against Unlawful Eviction

## Category: Housing & Tenancy Rights
## Citation: Model Tenancy Act, 2021 & State Rent Control Acts
## Statutory Urgency: High

### Legal Protection Against Arbitrary Eviction
Under Indian tenancy laws and the Model Tenancy Act, no landlord can forcibly evict a tenant or enter rented premises without due process of law, regardless of clauses inserted into informal lease agreements.

### Core Legal Rules:
1. **Mandatory Written Notice Period**: A landlord must provide at least 30 to 60 days of formal written notice stating valid grounds (such as non-payment of rent for consecutive months or bona fide self-use) before terminating a lease.
2. **Prohibition of Utility Cutoffs**: It is illegal for a landlord or property manager to cut off essential services (electricity, water supply, sewage, lift access, or parking access) to coerce a tenant into vacating or paying disputed charges. Doing so is an offense punishable by fines and immediate restoration orders by the Rent Authority / Civil Court.
3. **No Self-Help Lockouts**: Changing locks, dumping belongings, or intimidating tenants is criminal trespassing and intimidation under Sections 441 and 506 of the Indian Penal Code (or Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita).
4. **Valid Grounds for Eviction**: Non-payment of rent for over 2 months, subletting without consent, structural damage to the property, or misuse for illegal purposes.

### Immediate Action Checklist for Tenants:
- Preserve all rent payment proofs (bank transfer receipts, UPI references, or rent receipts).
- Communicate strictly in writing (email or registered WhatsApp) refusing unilateral eviction and citing tenancy agreement terms.
- If threatened with disconnection of water/electricity, file an emergency petition before the local Rent Authority or Rent Tribunal.
