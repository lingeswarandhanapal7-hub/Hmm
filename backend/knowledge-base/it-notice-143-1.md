# Income Tax Notice under Section 143(1) — Summary Intimation

## Category: Tax & Government Notices
## Citation: Income Tax Act, 1961 — Section 143(1)
## Statutory Urgency: Medium to High (30 days from receipt)

### What Section 143(1) Means
Section 143(1) is a computerized intimation sent to every taxpayer after their filed Income Tax Return (ITR) is processed by the Centralized Processing Center (CPC). It is NOT an audit, nor does it imply a tax penalty or criminal charge. It is a comparative mathematical reconciliation between what you reported and what the Income Tax Department calculated.

### Three Types of Section 143(1) Notices:
1. **No Demand / No Refund (Neutral)**: The department's calculation matches your return exactly. No action is required. Keep for records.
2. **Refund Determined**: The department found you overpaid tax or were eligible for higher tax credits (TDS/TCS). The refund will be credited directly to your pre-validated bank account.
3. **Demand Determined (Payment Required)**: The department calculated that you owe additional tax or interest (e.g. discrepancy in Form 26AS/AIS, disallowed deductions, or delayed filing interest under 234A/B/C).

### Statutory Deadlines & Next Steps:
- **30-Day Window**: If an additional tax demand is raised, you have strictly 30 days from the date of receiving the intimation to respond on the e-filing portal (https://eportal.incometax.gov.in).
- **If You Agree**: Pay the outstanding tax under Challan 280 (Self Assessment / Demand) and submit the payment acknowledgment reference on the portal.
- **If You Disagree**: Do NOT pay immediately. Select "Disagree with Demand" under Pending Actions > Response to Outstanding Demand, choose the specific discrepancy reason (e.g., TDS credit mismatch, double count), and submit rectification under Section 154.
- **Failure to Respond**: Unanswered demands after 30 days attract interest under Section 220 at 1% per month and can be adjusted automatically against future tax refunds.
