# Consumer Rights — Defective Goods, E-Commerce & Warranty Redressal

## Category: Consumer Rights & Commercial Protections
## Citation: Consumer Protection Act, 2019 & E-Commerce Rules, 2020
## Statutory Urgency: Medium (Statute of limitations: 2 years from cause of action)

### Fundamental Consumer Rights Under CPA 2019
The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 replaced the 1986 Act, introducing stringent penalties for misleading advertisements, unfair trade practices, and defective products with strict **Product Liability**.

### Core Guarantees:
1. **Right to Replacement or Full Refund**: If a product has an inherent manufacturing defect or fails to perform as promised within warranty, the seller/manufacturer cannot insist on endless repairs. The consumer is entitled to replacement or refund.
2. **E-Commerce Liability**: Online marketplaces (e.g. Amazon, Flipkart, Blinkit, Meesho) cannot escape liability by claiming to be mere intermediaries. Under the 2020 E-Commerce Rules, platforms must designate a Grievance Officer, acknowledge complaints within 48 hours, and resolve them within one month.
3. **No Unilateral "No Return / No Refund" Clauses**: A blanket "goods once sold cannot be returned" notice printed on a bill is an unfair trade practice under Section 2(47) and is legally unenforceable when goods are defective.

### Step-by-Step Redressal Protocol:
- **Step 1**: Register complaint on the National Consumer Helpline (NCH): Dial **1915** or WhatsApp to **8884000191** or portal at https://consumerhelpline.gov.in. NCH resolves over 85% of retail disputes at pre-litigation stage.
- **Step 2**: File an electronic case on **e-Daakhil** (https://edaakhil.nic.in) before the District Consumer Commission without hiring an expensive lawyer.
- **Step 3**: Pecuniary jurisdiction: Claims up to ₹50 Lakhs are heard by the District Commission.
