# Medical Lab Report Glossary — Blood Sugar (HbA1c) & Renal/Liver Function Tests

## Category: Medical Knowledge & Diagnostic Glossaries
## Clinical Safety Notice: Explanatory reference only. Never state a clinical diagnosis or treatment course. Direct patient to review with a licensed medical practitioner.
## Urgency: Routine / Informational

### Blood Sugar & Glycemic Markers
- **Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS)**: Glucose level measured after 8-10 hours overnight fasting. Normal reference: 70-99 mg/dL. 100-125 mg/dL indicates prediabetes range; 126+ mg/dL on repeated testing suggests diabetes.
- **Post-Prandial Blood Sugar (PPBS)**: Glucose level measured precisely 2 hours after the start of a meal. Normal: < 140 mg/dL.
- **HbA1c (Glycated Hemoglobin)**: Reflects average blood sugar levels over the past 90 days (the average lifespan of red blood cells).
  - Below 5.7%: Normal non-diabetic range.
  - 5.7% to 6.4%: Prediabetes range (lifestyle intervention window).
  - 6.5% or above: Consistent with diabetes mellitus.

### Kidney (Renal) Function Tests (KFT / RFT)
- **Serum Creatinine**: Byproduct of muscle metabolism filtered out almost entirely by healthy kidneys. Normal: 0.7 - 1.3 mg/dL (adult males), 0.6 - 1.1 mg/dL (adult females). High levels may indicate decreased kidney filtration efficiency.
- **Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN) / Blood Urea**: Waste product from protein breakdown. Normal: 7 - 20 mg/dL.
- **eGFR (Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate)**: Calculated efficiency score of kidney filtering capacity based on creatinine, age, and biological sex. > 90 mL/min is normal.

### Liver Function Tests (LFT)
- **SGPT / ALT & SGOT / AST**: Liver enzymes released into circulation when liver cells experience stress or inflammation.
- **Serum Bilirubin**: Breakdown product of red blood cells processed by liver; elevation causes jaundice (yellowing of eyes/skin).

### Patient Safety Action Points:
- Single abnormal readings can stem from hydration status, intense exercise, or medication side-effects.
- Never alter insulin or oral hypoglycemic dosages without direct medical consultation.
- Write down your current supplements and medications before discussing these lab results with your doctor.
