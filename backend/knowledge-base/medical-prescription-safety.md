# Medical Prescription Safety & Common Clinical Abbreviations

## Category: Medical Knowledge & Prescription Safety
## Clinical Safety Notice: Mandatory Patient Safety Mandate. Never instruct a patient to change, discontinue, or alter drug dosages or timing. Always state: "Confirm all medication regimens with your registered prescribing doctor or dispensing pharmacist."
## Urgency: Medium (Preventing medication errors)

### Common Prescription Abbreviations Decoded
- **OD (Once Daily)**: To be taken once every 24 hours. Check whether prescribed morning or bedtime.
- **BD / BID (Twice Daily)**: To be taken twice a day, spaced approximately 12 hours apart.
- **TDS / TID (Three Times Daily)**: To be taken 3 times a day, spaced approximately 8 hours apart.
- **QID (Four Times Daily)**: To be taken 4 times a day, spaced approximately 6 hours apart.
- **SOS / PRN (As Needed)**: Only taken when a specific symptom arises (e.g. fever above 101°F or severe acute pain), not on a continuous routine.
- **AC (Ante Cibum)**: Before food / on an empty stomach (usually 30 to 60 minutes before meals).
- **PC (Post Cibum)**: After food (with or immediately following meals) to prevent gastric irritation.
- **HS (Hora Somni)**: At bedtime.
- **Stat**: Immediately / single urgent initial dose.

### Critical Patient Safety Principles:
1. **Generic Salt vs Brand Name**: Prescriptions often list brand names (e.g., Crocin, Augmentin, Lipitor). The active therapeutic ingredient is the generic chemical molecule (e.g., Paracetamol, Amoxicillin-Clavulanate, Atorvastatin). Always verify generic chemical strength with your pharmacist when accepting substitute brands.
2. **Antibiotic Course Completion**: If an antibiotic is prescribed for 5 or 7 days, halting early because you feel better fosters drug-resistant bacteria. Always discuss duration with your doctor.
3. **Food and Drug Interactions**: Certain medications interact strongly with dairy (calcium binds certain antibiotics), grapefruit juice, or antacids. Ask the pharmacist: "Should I take this with water, milk, or with meals?"
4. **Allergy Disclosure**: Ensure the doctor and chemist know of any prior allergic reactions to penicillin, sulfa drugs, or NSAIDs.
